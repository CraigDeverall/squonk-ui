# Motion

Motion is small and purposeful. It shows where something came from or went,
keeps your place when things change, and draws the eye to what just arrived. It
is never decoration that makes someone wait.

## How the system fits together

Four layers, each owned by one place, so they never fight:

| Layer | What it decides | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Character** | How motion feels: tempo, curves, how far things travel, bounce, stagger. | The theme: motion primitives, and the `roles.motion.*` purposes below. |
| **Vocabulary** | What kinds of motion exist: pop, slide, collapse, reveal, swap, glide… | The [Motion components](?path=/docs/animation-motion--docs). |
| **Choreography** | How several things move together. | `Motion.Stagger`, and the Layout and Presence components. |
| **Level** | How much motion there is: full, reduced or none. | The device's reduced-motion setting, and `SquonkThemeProvider`'s `motion` prop. |

The library's components already use the Motion components, so a theme
changes how every menu, dialog, toast, tab, table and accordion moves, and an
app's motion level applies to all of them.

## Every theme, the same scene

Press **Play** to run the same scene in each example theme: cards arriving in
sequence, a figure counting to its new value and highlighting, a list
reordering, and a popover under **Details**. Only the theme's motion character
differs: Fieldbook is instant and linear, Tidepool springs, Kauri fades slowly, Monolith slides all at once and Aurora glides. Use **Slow motion** to study the
curves, and **Reduced motion** to see what someone who asks for less motion
gets: fades in place, nothing travelling.

## Character: the theme's motion primitives

A theme sets its motion with six primitives. Every motion duration, curve and
distance derives from them, through the purpose tokens below.

| Primitive | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `transitionDurationMs` | `200` | The tempo. Overlays take this long; arrivals and panels 1.5×; counting figures 2.5×. |
| `motionTravelPx` | `16` | How far things move and grow as they arrive. Overlays grow from `1 − travel ÷ 400` of their size, content rises 1.5 × travel. `0` makes every arrival a plain fade. |
| `motionStaggerMs` | `40` | The gap between items arriving in sequence. |
| `motionBounce` | `0` | How much arrivals overshoot and settle, from 0 (none) to 1. Drawn as a CSS `linear()` spring, so it costs no JavaScript. Off by default: use it for a playful brand. |
| `motionEaseEnter`, `motionEaseExit`, `motionEaseStandard` | Decelerate, accelerate, both | The curves things arrive, leave and move within the page on: a keyword, `cubic-bezier()` or `linear()`. |

## Timing by purpose

Components time their movement from motion purposes, so menus, dialogs and
panels all move alike. Things arrive a little slower than they leave, so the
eye can follow them in. Re-point any of them with `roleBindings`, like
`{ "roles.motion.revealEasing": "motion.easings.spring" }`.

## Level: how much motion

People who ask their device for reduced motion get it everywhere, with nothing
to set up. Nothing travels or grows: overlays, drawers and content fade in
place; list items move at once; decorative loops stop; spinners slow down.
Colour changes, highlights and fades stay, because they don't move anything.

An app can also choose the level itself:

<InlineCodeExample>
{`// An in-app "Reduce motion" setting
<SquonkThemeProvider motion={settings.reduceMotion ? "reduced" : "system"}>

// Screenshots and tests: every change is immediate, loops hold still
<SquonkThemeProvider motion="none">`}
</InlineCodeExample>

`system` (the default) follows the device. `reduced` reduces motion whatever
the device says. `none` turns motion off; it's for tests and screenshots, not
people. The level is published as `--squonk-motion-level-*` custom properties, so
it applies before the page hydrates. Set them on an element to scope a level,
or slow motion (`--squonk-motion-level-time: 4`), to part of a page, as the lab
above does.

## Reveal and stagger

`Motion.Reveal` fades and rises content into place as it scrolls into view.
Inside `Motion.Stagger`, each item waits one stagger step more than the one
before, so a group reads as a sequence. Its timing is the `reveal`,
`revealEasing`, `revealOffset` and `stagger` purposes.

## Rules

- **Respect the motion level.** Never force motion past it: take timing from
  the Motion components and they follow it for you.
- **Never hide content behind motion.** Animated content ends visible and
  readable, and stays available if the animation never runs. Figures that count
  are read out once, at their final value.
- **Animate arrivals and changes, not work.** Page sections, cards and
  notifications arrive with motion; a list re-sorting glides; a recalculated
  total highlights. Fields and text someone is reading or typing into don't
  move.
- **Subtle by default.** A few pixels and a few hundred milliseconds. Bounce is
  a brand choice, off unless a theme asks for it, and never on drawers or
  anything people work in.
- **Keep timing in the library.** Use the components' own motion, or wrap your
  own element in a Motion component. A one-off transition in product code
  drifts from the rest; new motion belongs in the library as a Motion component
  or a motion purpose.
