# Popover

<ResizableCanvas of={PopoverStories.Default} defaultHeight={300} />

`Popover` shows a small panel next to the control that opened it: an
explanation with a link, a few filters, a person's contact details. Unlike a
`Tooltip`, it opens on a click and can hold links, buttons and form fields.
Unlike a `Modal`, the page behind stays usable and it closes when people click
elsewhere.

```tsx
import { Popover } from "@squonk/ui/popover";

<Popover trigger={<Button>What's included?</Button>} title="Included in your dues">
  <Text>Annual dues, a late fee and the Swamp Fund levy to today.</Text>
</Popover>
```

Use a `Modal` when people must finish or cancel before carrying on, a `Drawer`
for longer side tasks, and a `DropdownMenu` for a list of actions.

## The trigger

`trigger` is one focusable element that accepts a `ref`, usually a `Button` or
`IconButton`. The popover adds `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` to it.

## Arrow and placement

`placement` is a preference; the panel flips when there is no room. `arrow` adds

## Accessibility

- The panel is a non-modal `dialog`, named by `title` and described by
  `description`. Without a title, give it an `aria-label`.
- Opening moves focus into the panel; Escape, the close button or a click outside
  closes it and returns focus to the trigger.
- The panel is portalled so it is never clipped. While it is open, `Tab` stays
  within the panel and the rest of the page is hidden from assistive
  technology, so close it to move on.

## Common pitfalls

- **Don't use a Popover for essential steps.** It closes when people click away.
- **Don't open a Popover on hover.** Use a `Tooltip` for hover hints.
- **Don't put a long form in a Popover.** It scrolls in a small box; use a `Drawer`.
