# Dashboards and summaries

An overview screen answers "what needs my attention, and how are things going?"
in that order. It is usually the first page of an app, so it sets the tone: lead
with what people must do, then the few figures they act on, then a way into the
detail. Build it from [Alert](?path=/docs/functional-alert--docs),
[Stat](?path=/docs/functional-stat--docs), [Card](?path=/docs/layout-card--docs),
[Grid](?path=/docs/layout-primitives-grid--docs) and, where a trend matters,
the charts.

## What goes where

| Part | Use | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Something that needs action soon | An [Alert](?path=/docs/functional-alert--docs) at the top, with a link to do it | One or two. If everything is urgent, nothing is |
| The figures people act on | A row of [Stat](?path=/docs/functional-stat--docs)s in a `Grid` of Cards | Three or four. Add `change` only when the comparison means something |
| A trend behind a figure | Stat `trend`, a [Sparkline](?path=/docs/functional-sparkline--docs), or a chart in its own Card below | See [Data visualization](?path=/docs/patterns-data-visualization--docs) |
| Recent activity | A short list in a Card, with "See all" | Five items or so; the full list lives on its own page |
| Ways into common tasks | [InteractiveCard](?path=/docs/layout-interactive-card--docs)s or plain links | Only the tasks most people do most often |
| A level against a limit | [Meter](?path=/docs/functional-meter--docs) | Say the level in words as well as colour |

## Summaries of one thing

A summary page for one record (a membership account, a case, a member) follows the
same order on a smaller scale: its status as a [Badge](?path=/docs/functional-badge--docs)
next to its name, an Alert if something needs doing, the key figures, then
details grouped under headings, often in [Tabs](?path=/docs/functional-tabs--docs)
when the views are separate ("Summary", "Payments", "History").

## Before a big action

A review step before submitting ("Check your answers") is a summary too: group
the answers under the same headings as the form, show each value as people will
see it, and give each group a "Change" link back to the step. See
[Multi-step forms](?path=/docs/patterns-forms-multi-step-forms--docs).

## Accessibility

- Give the page one `h1`, and a heading for each card (`Heading level` to match
  the page's outline). Charts in cards set `hideTitle` so the heading isn't
  repeated.
- Stat and Sparkline describe their figures and direction in words; don't add a
  second description.
- An Alert that appears as the page loads isn't announced as news; that's
  intended. Don't move focus to it.
- Keep the reading order the order of importance: attention, figures, detail.

## Pitfalls

- **A wall of numbers.** Every figure should change what someone does. Leave the
  rest for a report.
- **Status colours as decoration.** Green and red mean good and bad news; don't
  use them to tell figures apart.
- **Stale figures without a date.** Say when the data was last updated if it
  isn't live.
- **Empty dashboards on day one.** A new account has nothing to summarise yet;
  show set-up steps instead. See
  [Onboarding and first run](?path=/docs/patterns-onboarding-and-first-run--docs).
