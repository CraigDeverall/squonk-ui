# Data visualization

Start from the question people are asking of the data, then pick the form that
answers it. Every chart in the library shares one frame: a required title, a
table view of the same data, a legend once there are two series, a tooltip you
can reach by keyboard, and one categorical palette. Your app owns the data, its
formatting and the words.

## Choosing a form

| The question | Use |
| --- | --- |
| How many, how much, right now? | [Stat](?path=/docs/functional-stat--docs). Sometimes the right chart is a number. |
| Which way has this figure been heading? | [Sparkline](?path=/docs/functional-sparkline--docs) beside the figure, or a Stat with `trend` |
| How did it change over time? | [LineChart](?path=/docs/functional-line-chart--docs); [AreaChart](?path=/docs/functional-area-chart--docs) when the total volume matters, stacked to show what it is made of |
| How do these categories compare? | [BarChart](?path=/docs/functional-bar-chart--docs); horizontal for a ranking or long category names |
| What is the whole made of? | [DonutChart](?path=/docs/functional-donut-chart--docs) for two to five parts; a BarChart for more, or when parts need comparing with each other |
| Do these two measures move together? | [ScatterChart](?path=/docs/functional-scatter-chart--docs) |
| How full is it, against a known limit? | [Meter](?path=/docs/functional-meter--docs) |
| How far along is this task? | [ProgressBar](?path=/docs/functional-progress-bar--docs). It tracks work toward done, not a measure, so it carries no warning levels. |
| How are these things connected to each other? | [NetworkGraph](?path=/docs/functional-network-graph--docs), for up to about 150 things linked many-to-many. To look one of them up, use a Table. |
| What exactly is the value for this row? | [Table](?path=/docs/functional-table--docs). If people will look values up, a chart is the wrong tool. |

A chart shows shape: rising, falling, bigger, smaller, clustered. If no one will
act on the shape, show the numbers.

### Against a limit, and toward done

[Meter](?path=/docs/functional-meter--docs) shows a measure against a known
limit, with warning and critical levels.
[ProgressBar](?path=/docs/functional-progress-bar--docs) shows a task moving
toward done. They look alike, so pick by what the bar means.

<Example caption="The meter turns to warning past 48 GB; the progress bar has no levels, only how far the task has got.">
  <Card>
    <VStack gap="lg">
      <Meter label="Field-photo storage" value={51} maxValue={60} valueLabel="51 of 60 GB" showValue warningAt={48} criticalAt={57} />
      <ProgressBar label="Importing the 2026 sighting log" value={64} showValue />
    </VStack>
  </Card>
</Example>

### Connections

[NetworkGraph](?path=/docs/functional-network-graph--docs) shows how things link
to each other, many-to-many: clusters, hubs and things that stand alone. Give it
a [NetworkGraphLegend](?path=/docs/functional-network-graph--docs) built from
the same `nodeKinds` and `linkKinds`, so the key always matches the drawing.
Shape, not only colour, tells the kinds apart.

      links={chapterLinks}

## Colour

- Series take the eight palette slots (`--squonk-roles-color-data-series1`…`8`) **in
  order**. The order is fixed and checked for colour-vision deficiencies, so
  don't reorder it or pick colours by taste.
- Colour follows the series, never its position. When a series can be filtered
  out, or the same measure appears in several charts on a page, pin it with
  `slot` so it keeps its colour. A Sparkline or Stat trend that shows the same
  measure takes the same slot.
- Eight series is the most one chart colours apart. Before that, fold the small
  ones into "Other" (DonutChart does this past seven parts) or split the chart
  into small multiples.
- Status colours (success, warning, error) are reserved for good and bad news:
  Stat's change and Meter's levels. Don't use them as series colours.
- Text is never drawn in a series colour. Labels, legends and tooltips use text
  colours and a swatch beside them, so they stay readable for everyone.

## One axis per chart

Charts have one value axis. When two measures have different units or scales
(dues collected and the number of sightings, say), draw **two charts** side by
side with the same x axis, rather than one chart with an axis on each side. Dual
axes invite people to read meaning into where the lines cross, and that point
depends only on how the axes were scaled.

## Dashboards

Lead with a row of Stats in a [Grid](?path=/docs/layout-primitives-grid--docs) of
[Cards](?path=/docs/layout-card--docs): the three or four figures people act on.
Charts that explain them go below, each in its own Card. Give each card a
`Heading` at the right level for the page and set `hideTitle` on the chart, so the
heading isn't repeated; the chart's `title` still names it for screen readers and
captions its table view.

## Accessibility

- **Every chart has a title**, even when `hideTitle` hides it. It names the chart
  for assistive technology and captions the table view. Say what is shown and
  over what ("Sightings logged by chapter, 2026"), not "Chart".
- **Table view.** Every chart has a "Table view" toggle that shows the same data
  as a [Table](?path=/docs/functional-table--docs). It serves screen reader users,
  people who need exact values and anyone who can't separate the colours. Don't
  hide it, and don't build a second table beside the chart.
- **Keyboard.** Tab to the plot: focus shows the first point, and the left and
  right arrow keys move the tooltip from point to point, as hover does with a
  mouse. Horizontal bar charts aren't a focus stop, because the arrow keys
  can't step through them; there the table view is the keyboard route, as it
  is for exact values in every chart.
- **Legend.** A chart with two or more series shows a legend of swatches and
  names in text colour. Pair colour with something else where you can: direct
  labels, the order of the legend matching the order of the marks, or a split
  into separate charts.
- Sparkline and Stat speak their first and last values and the direction of
  change in words; Meter announces its `valueLabel`. Say warning and critical
  levels in words as well as colour.
