# Onboarding and first run

The first visit is the screen with the least in it and the most riding on it.
Don't explain the app; get people to the first useful thing. Show what will
appear and offer the first step, then step out of the way. Compose
[EmptyState](?path=/docs/layout-empty-state--docs),
[Illustration](?path=/docs/components-text-and-icons-illustration--docs),
[ProgressBar](?path=/docs/functional-progress-bar--docs),
[Card](?path=/docs/layout-card--docs) and [Button](?path=/docs/functional-button--docs).

## Which approach

| The situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| An area that's empty until people add something | A first-use [EmptyState](?path=/docs/layout-empty-state--docs): what will appear here, and one primary action to start |
| Several set-up steps people can do in any order, over days | A set-up checklist in a Card on the overview, with progress |
| Steps that must happen in order before the app works | A short [multi-step form](?path=/docs/patterns-forms-multi-step-forms--docs), then the app |
| A new feature in an app people already know | An inline [Alert](?path=/docs/functional-alert--docs) `tone="info"` they can dismiss (`onDismiss`), next to the feature |
| A term or field people may not understand | `helperText`, or a [Popover](?path=/docs/functional-popover--docs) with a link to more |

## First use

A first-use empty state says what the area is for and offers one way to start.
An [Illustration](?path=/docs/components-text-and-icons-illustration--docs) makes
the moment friendlier; leave it out in small spaces. Make the action specific
("Log your first sighting", not "Get started"). See
[Empty and error states](?path=/docs/patterns-empty-and-error-states--docs).

## Set-up checklists

<Example caption="Progress in words and a bar, done steps marked, and the next step as the one primary action.">
  <SetupChecklistExample />
</Example>

- **Say how far along people are** in words ("2 of 4 done") as well as the bar.
- **Offer the next step** as the one primary action; the rest stay in the list.
- **Let people leave and come back.** Keep progress on the server, not in the
  browser.
- **Go away when it's done.** Thank them once, then remove the checklist.

## Tours and walkthroughs

The library has no tour or coach-mark component, on purpose. Overlays that
point at parts of the screen hide what they explain, are hard to use with a
keyboard or a screen reader, and are usually skipped. Put the explanation where
it's needed: a clear label, help text, an empty state that teaches by doing.

## Accessibility

- A checklist is an ordered list (`<ol>`); mark done steps with text or an icon
  with a `label`, not with colour or strikethrough alone.
- Don't move focus to welcome messages or dismissible Alerts on load.
- A dismissed message stays dismissed: remember it on the server.

## Pitfalls

- **A welcome modal before anything else.** It blocks the first task to talk
  about it.
- **Empty dashboards.** A new account's overview shows set-up, not zeros.
- **Asking for everything up front.** Ask only what the first task needs; the
  rest can wait until it matters.
- **Onboarding that never ends.** Every hint has an end: when it's done, or when
  it's dismissed.
