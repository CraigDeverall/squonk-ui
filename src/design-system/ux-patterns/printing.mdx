# Printing

Some pages are printed: statements, receipts, sighting reports, newsletters, a plan to sign.
The library prints every page well without any work: ink on white, no shadows
or motion, no buttons, tables in full and link addresses shown. For a page
people print on purpose, decide what belongs on paper with
[Printable, NotPrintable and PrintOnly](?path=/docs/style-printable--docs).

## The rules

- **Mark the record, not the rest.** Wrap what someone prints the page for in
  `Printable`: the statement, the receipt, the schedule. Everything else is left
  off without listing it.
- **Leave the screen's machinery off.** Navigation, filters, actions and help
  that only work on screen go in `NotPrintable` when the whole page prints.
- **Say on paper what the screen said another way.** A reference number, the
  date and time printed, who it's for, or a line replacing a control ("Pay
  online at …") go in `PrintOnly`.
- **Keep what belongs together on one page** with `keepTogether`, and start a
  new section on a fresh page with `breakBefore`.
- **Never print secrets the screen masked**: a printed page leaves your app's
  control.

## Choosing a mark

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Print only part of the page | `Printable` around that part |
| Print the page without something | `NotPrintable` around that thing |
| Add something only to the printout | `PrintOnly` |
| Start a section on a new page | `<Printable breakBefore>` |
| Stop a block splitting across pages | `<Printable keepTogether>` |
| Mark part of a sentence | Any of them with `inline` |

## The theme on paper

The theme decides how paper looks. Its `printBackgrounds` switch keeps the
theme's own colours and backgrounds (for a branded receipt, say); left off, a
page prints in ink on white, whatever the theme or mode. `printLinkUrls`
(on by default) prints each web or email link's address after its text. The
colours paper uses are purpose tokens under `roles.print`, which a theme can
re-point one by one. See [Theme](?path=/docs/foundations-theme--docs).

## What your app owns

The browser owns the page size, the margins it offers, and the header and
footer it prints (the title, the address and page numbers), which readers can
turn off. Test a real printout or its PDF, in more than one browser, for any
page people rely on printing.
