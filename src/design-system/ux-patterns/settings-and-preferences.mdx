# Settings and preferences

A settings page is a set of small, separate decisions that people come back to
rarely and want to get right. Group them by topic, make it obvious when each
change takes effect, and keep the risky ones apart. Compose
[Heading](?path=/docs/functional-primitives-heading--docs)s,
[Switch](?path=/docs/functional-switch--docs),
[RadioGroup](?path=/docs/functional-radio-group--docs),
[Select](?path=/docs/functional-select--docs),
[Fieldset](?path=/docs/functional-fieldset--docs) and
[Button](?path=/docs/functional-button--docs).

## Saving: at once, or with a button

| The setting… | Use | Saves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turns one thing on or off ("Email reminders") | [Switch](?path=/docs/functional-switch--docs) | At once. Say "Saved", or move it back and say why if it fails |
| Is one choice from a few, with no side effects ("Theme: light, dark, system") | [RadioGroup](?path=/docs/functional-radio-group--docs) or [Select](?path=/docs/functional-select--docs) | At once, the same way |
| Is several fields that only make sense together (an address, a bank account) | A small form in its own section | With a button that names it: "Save address" |
| Is text people type | [TextField](?path=/docs/functional-text-field--docs) in a section form | With the section's button, never on each keystroke |
| Is hard to undo (cancel the membership, stop all newsletters) | A Button `danger` in its own section, last | After a [ConfirmModal](?path=/docs/functional-modal--docs) that names the consequence |

Say at the top of each section which kind it is ("These change as soon as you
switch them", "Saved when you press the button"). Never mix the two inside one
group. See [Switches, checkboxes and saving](?path=/docs/patterns-switches-checkboxes-and-saving--docs).

## Laying out a settings page

- **Group by topic**, each group a section with a heading and a one-line
  description: Contact details, Notifications, Security, Danger zone.
- **Long pages get a side navigation.** With more than five or six groups, add a
  [NavList](?path=/docs/components-navigation-navlist--docs) of the sections, or
  split them into pages under a settings section of the app.
- **One column.** Settings read top to bottom; keep them in a column capped at a
  readable width.
- **Destructive settings go last**, in their own section, with the consequence
  in words.

## Labels and help

Label the setting, not its state: "Email reminders", not "Reminders are on".
Use `helperText` for what changes ("Before each due date. You'll still get
notices.") and for anything people might worry about. See
[Writing for the interface](?path=/docs/patterns-writing-for-the-interface--docs).

## Accessibility

- Each section is a `<section>` named by its heading (`aria-labelledby`), so
  screen-reader users can move between groups.
- A switch that saves at once announces the result near itself (`role="status"`),
  not in a toast only.
- Related fields share a [Fieldset](?path=/docs/functional-fieldset--docs) with a
  legend that states the question.
- Don't disable a Save button to show nothing has changed; let it say "No
  changes to save" if pressed.

## Pitfalls

- **One Save button for the whole page.** People change one thing, scroll, and
  leave without saving. Save per section, or at once.
- **Switches in a form that saves later.** People can't tell whether anything
  changed.
- **Settings in a modal.** A settings page people return to deserves its own
  URL; use a modal only for one quick change.
- **Hidden consequences.** If a setting affects other people (a household member's
  access), say so beside it.

The [Account settings](?path=/story/examples-account-settings--overview)
example shows each kind: a contact form with an error summary, switches that
save at once (one fails and moves back), statements saved by a button, signed-in
devices with a menu, and a confirmation that can't be dismissed while it works.
