# Switches, checkboxes and saving

A [Switch](?path=/docs/functional-switch--docs) turns something **on or off**.
A [Checkbox](?path=/docs/functional-checkbox--docs) **selects** something. The
difference is what the user expects to happen when they press it.

## When a change takes effect

**Our guidance** (the sources describe the controls but not this timing rule):

- Use a **Switch** when the change applies straight away: "Email notifications",
  "Dark mode". Show that it worked, or that it failed, right there. If saving
  fails, move the switch back and say why.
- Use a **Checkbox** when the choice is part of something the user submits later:
  "I agree to the terms", "Send me a copy", or rows to act on. Nothing happens
  until they press the form's button.
- Don't mix the two in one form. A switch inside a form that only saves on
  Submit makes people unsure whether anything changed.

## Labels describe the setting, not the state

The label stays the same when the switch flips: "Email notifications", not
"Notifications are on". The switch itself shows the state. Use `helperText` for
consequences ("You'll still get security alerts").

## Errors

A setting that applies immediately reports failure where it happened, not as a
form error. A checkbox that must be ticked to continue gets an `error` like any
other form field.
